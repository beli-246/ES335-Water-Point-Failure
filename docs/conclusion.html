Conclusion

Static attributes rank rural water points by no-water risk better than a one-rule baseline in all four countries, and the gain is statistically clear at the district level. But the skill depends on having labelled data from the same country. A model trained on the other three countries' surveys does much worse, and ranks Ethiopia backwards. Pooling foreign data with local data only works if the model is told the country, because some features mean opposite things in different places; even then it only matches local-only training.

A ministry should label a sample of its own water points before trusting a model. In Ethiopia, Kenya and Tanzania, 14,000–19,000 local labels beat a model trained on 134,000–140,000 points from elsewhere. For the method, the lessons are the ones the course stresses, measured here: validate on held-out groups when data is clustered (random CV overstated PR-AUC by 50%), compare against a baseline with a paired test, choose models without looking at the test set, and check which "signal" is really the data-collection process. That last check matters here: Ethiopia's strongest result turned out to be mostly a survey-program effect.
