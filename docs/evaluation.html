Evaluation design

Test set: whole districts, frozen before any model was trained

Random row splits put a pump's neighbours in training and the pump in test, so the model is graded on places it has effectively seen [Roberts et al. 2017]. Our concept experiment measures the effect: on Uganda, random 3-fold CV says PR-AUC 0.43, but holding out whole districts says 0.28. So, per country, we hold out whole districts (district = region + district name, because the same district name occurs in two regions) covering about 20% of rows. Among 5,000 seeded random draws, we pick the one whose no-water rate is closest to the country's, which is a stratified group split. Districts with more than 15% of a country's rows are never test districts; Ethiopia's largest has 29.3%. The list was committed to the repository (splits/test_districts.json) before any model was fitted.

Three training regimes, one test set

  • A · pooled: the other three countries plus the target's training districts.
  • B · foreign only: the other three countries. The model has never seen the target country.
  • C · local only: the target's training districts.

All three are scored on the same test districts, so differences between them are paired. Regime C was not in the original plan. We added it after the first run (see the amendment), when pooled logistic regression ranked Ethiopia backwards.

Metrics

  • The primary metric is PR-AUC (average precision) of the no-water class. A crew acts on the top of the ranking, and PR-AUC measures how pure the top is [Saito & Rehmsmeier 2015]. A random ranking scores the no-water rate, which runs from 9% (Kenya) to 43% (Tanzania), so raw PR-AUC cannot be compared across countries.
  • To compare countries we use skill. Lift = PR-AUC ÷ no-water rate is intuitive ("2× better than random"), but its ceiling is 1 ÷ no-water rate: 11× in Kenya, only 2.3× in Tanzania. A perfect Tanzanian model would look worse than a mediocre Kenyan one. Skill = (PR-AUC − rate) ÷ (1 − rate) is 0 for random and 1 for perfect, whatever the base rate. It is the same normalisation our tuning score uses.
  • We do not use accuracy. In Kenya, "every point works" is 91% accurate and finds zero broken points. Accuracy rewards the majority class, so it is useless for ranking.
  • We also report ROC-AUC; precision and recall when a crew inspects the top 5/10/20% (tie-aware: the one-rule stump gives only two scores, so ties are broken at random in expectation); and the Brier score and a reliability curve for calibration.

Tuning and uncertainty

Hyperparameters are chosen by grouped 3-fold cross-validation over training districts only. The test districts are scored once, at the end. The tuning score is computed within each country and averaged. A pooled score over a fold that mixes Tanzania (43%) with Kenya (9%) mostly rewards telling the countries apart, which is not the task.

For uncertainty we resample test districts (not rows) 2,000 times [Efron & Tibshirani 1993]. Because two regimes are scored on the same districts, we bootstrap the difference directly (a paired bootstrap). Checking whether two separate confidence intervals overlap is a much weaker test.

On 6 Nov we amended the plan and added regime C. The first run gave pooled logistic regression an Ethiopia ROC-AUC of 0.38, worse than random. Its coefficients flip sign between the pooled fit and an Ethiopia-only fit. For example, a missing install year means low risk in Kenya, where most such points come from one well-performing provider, but high risk in Ethiopia, where they come from the Ministry. A single set of coefficients cannot fit both, so the pooled model buries the local signal (a Simpson's-paradox pattern; in dataset-shift terms, the relationship between inputs and label changes between countries [Moreno-Torres et al. 2012]). This diagnostic scored the test districts once, but the decision can be made on training districts alone: in-sample ROC-AUC on Ethiopia's training points is 0.83 for an Ethiopia-only fit against 0.67 pooled. Test districts, features, metrics and tuning did not change.

On 15 Nov we amended the plan again. A review of our own design pointed out that regime A cannot see the country, so it is a weak test of pooling. We added pooled models that know the country (results): country as an input for trees and boosting, and country × every feature for logistic regression. We also ran the planned "Uganda subsampled" ablation, which had been dropped for time. The reason for both is visible without any test result. Test districts, features, metrics, tuning and the final-model choice did not change.
