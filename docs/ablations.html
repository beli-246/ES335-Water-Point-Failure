Ablations: is the signal real?

  • Removing the missing-value signal asks whether the model ranks points by survey provider, through which values are missing.
  • Removing remoteness and population asks whether the local advantage survives without them. These features locate a point, and the country is 97.5% recoverable from the full feature set.
  • Adding fee payment tests a predictor from earlier work: across 25,000 hand pumps in Liberia, Sierra Leone and Uganda, not collecting user fees was one of the factors linked to non-functioning pumps, alongside age and distance from the district capital [Foster 2013]. In WPdx, payment is recorded almost only in Tanzania.
  • Cutting Uganda to 21,000 training rows (about the size of each other country) asks whether Uganda, which is 60% of all rows, swamps the foreign data, and whether that is why foreign-only training fails.

Measured as test skill (0 = random) for XGBoost and logistic regression in all three regimes:

  • Without the missing-value signal, Ethiopia's local XGBoost falls from 0.30 to 0.19 and Uganda's local logistic regression from 0.17 to 0.09. Part of the signal in those countries is which provider surveyed the point. Kenya and Tanzania barely move. Local models still beat foreign-only ones everywhere, so the local advantage survives without fingerprinting.
  • Without remoteness and population, Uganda and Tanzania lose the most (local XGBoost 0.19 → 0.13 and 0.34 → 0.28): location features carry real signal there. The local-beats-foreign gap survives in every country.
  • Fee payment helps where it is recorded (Tanzania local XGBoost 0.34 → 0.37) and helps more in Uganda (0.19 → 0.28) and pooled Ethiopia. Payment is missing for 64% of Ugandan and 91% of Ethiopian points, so those gains may again partly be missingness. It is a lead for future data collection; it does not show that collecting fees keeps pumps working.
  • With Uganda cut to 21,000 rows, regimes A and B change by at most 0.03 PR-AUC for Ethiopia, Kenya and Tanzania, mostly within noise (results/uganda_sub_vs_main.csv), and foreign-only training still ranks Ethiopia worse than random (ROC-AUC 0.33–0.41 for the tuned models). So foreign-only training fails because the foreign surveys differ, not because one large country swamps them.
