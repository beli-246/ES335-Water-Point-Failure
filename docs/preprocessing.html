Preprocessing

Every transform lives inside a scikit-learn Pipeline and is fitted on the training folds only, so no statistic from validation or test points leaks into training (src/features.py).

  • Distances and population are heavy-tailed (a few points are 100+ km from a road), so they get log1p. This matters for linear models and SVMs, whose loss is dominated by extreme values; trees split on order and do not care.
  • Only logistic regression and the SVMs get scaled inputs (z-scores): the regularisation penalty and the RBF kernel distance both assume features on comparable scales.
  • Categoricals are one-hot encoded. Levels with fewer than 30 training rows are merged into "infrequent". A level first seen at test time goes into that group when it exists, and otherwise gets all-zero columns. Why 30: at Kenya's 9% no-water rate, a level with 30 points has about 3 dry ones, so its coefficient or split would be fitted to two or three examples, which is pure variance.
  • Class imbalance (9–43% no water) is not reweighted. Our metrics judge the order of points, and class weights mainly shift predicted probabilities. We checked this with 5-fold grouped CV on each country's training districts: balanced weights for logistic regression and scale_pos_weight for XGBoost changed CV PR-AUC by at most 0.014, well inside the fold-to-fold spread of 0.03–0.11 (src/extra_checks.py).
  • Missing numbers get the median plus a 0/1 "was missing" column, and missing categories get a "missing" level. The no-missing-signal ablation imputes without any flag, so no model can see that a value was absent. That includes XGBoost, which would otherwise learn from NaN.
  • Install year is set to missing when before 1950 or after the survey year (365 values). WPdx's own duplicate flag removes 1.1% of rows.
