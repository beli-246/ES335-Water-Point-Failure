Data

Water Point Data Exchange Plus (WPdx+), CC BY 4.0, downloaded 6 November 2026 [WPdx]. One row per water point at its latest survey.

What a full-column audit found before any modelling

We profiled every one of the 74 columns before training a model. Script: src/audit_data.py; report: results/audit/data_audit.md.

  • Several columns leak the label. water_point_history separates working from broken points with AUC 0.99, the raw status text 0.96, and status_clean is the label in other words. All are excluded. So are the dates and survey IDs: source alone scores 0.85 in Ethiopia because different surveys found very different failure rates.
  • Missing values are not random. In Kenya, half the rows come from one provider whose points are 95% missing an install year and only 1.6% broken. In Ethiopia, the Ministry's rows are 100% missing a year and 38% broken. "Year is missing" therefore predicts the label (AUC 0.67 in Kenya, 0.63 in Ethiopia), but what it predicts is who did the survey. We keep the missing-value flags in the main models and remove them in an ablation.
  • The country is recoverable from the features. A boosted-tree classifier names the country from our input features alone with 97.5% accuracy (majority baseline 60%). So "transfer to an unseen country" is really transfer to an unseen survey, and we word every conclusion that way.
  • 3,397 rows (2.1%) have status "Unknown" and are dropped, because they have no label. They are not spread evenly: 63% come from one provider, Evidence Action, whose labelled points are almost all working (1.6% no water in Kenya, 0% in Uganda), and Tanzania has none. Even in the extreme case where every Unknown point was working, the no-water rate would fall by at most 0.4 percentage points (Uganda; 0.1–0.3 in Ethiopia and Kenya), so dropping them cannot change the conclusions.
  • Points cluster in space. District identity alone predicts the label (AUC 0.65–0.76), so neighbouring points are not independent. This decides the test split described under Evaluation design.
