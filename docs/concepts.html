Concepts, shown on our data

Each panel below is a course concept, measured on Uganda's training districts: models are fitted on some training districts and scored on other, held-out training districts. The frozen test districts are never touched.

Logistic regression from scratch: first-order vs second-order optimisation

We minimise the L2-regularised cross-entropy J(w,b) = −(1/n) Σ [y log p + (1−y) log(1−p)] + (λ/2)‖w‖², with gradient (1/n) Xᵀ(p − y) + λw. Gradient descent uses only the slope. Newton's method (IRLS) also uses the curvature, through the Hessian (1/n) Xᵀ diag(p(1−p)) X + λI, and jumps straight to the bottom of the bowl. The loss is convex, so there is one optimum, the same one scikit-learn finds by solving the problem with C = 1/(nλ). Newton reaches it in 5 steps; gradient descent is still slightly short after 3,000 (largest coefficient gap 0.03), though its predictions are almost identical.

Regularisation: L1 selects, L2 shrinks

Weakening the penalty (moving right) lets coefficients grow. Under L1 they enter one at a time from exactly zero, which is feature selection. Under L2 all of them grow smoothly and none is exactly zero. The three largest coefficients are coloured.

Bias–variance: pruning a decision tree

Cost-complexity pruning

An unpruned tree memorises training districts (training PR-AUC 1.0) but scores only 0.18 on held-out districts. Moderate pruning lifts that to 0.25 (α = 0.0003); too much pruning makes the tree too simple and it falls back to 0.18. Variance on the left, bias on the right.

Boosting overfits with rounds and depth; the RBF SVM trades margin against kernel width

XGBoost, learning rate 0.05

Training PR-AUC keeps climbing. For deep trees, held-out PR-AUC peaks early (round 32 at depth 6, round 69 at depth 12) and then falls, while depth-2 trees keep improving slowly for over 1,000 rounds; deeper trees widen the train–held-out gap. That is why depth, child weight and λ are tuned.

RBF SVM: C × γ on 15,000 points

Large γ makes each point's kernel narrow (wiggly boundary, high variance); small C gives a softer margin (more bias). The best cell is bold.
