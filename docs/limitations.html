Limitations

  • The label is one survey day. "No water" mixes broken pumps, seasonally dry sources and differences in survey practice. We predict what a survey would find, not mechanical failure.
  • Country and survey are confounded. The country is 97.5% recoverable from the features, and each country's data comes from a few providers and years (73% of Uganda's rows were reported in a single year). "Transfer to a new country" here means transfer to a new survey and era; we cannot separate the two.
  • Missingness is informative and provider-driven (missing not at random). It helps the models in Ethiopia and Uganda, and it would not help in a place surveyed differently. The ablation measures how much it contributes.
  • Ethiopia's headline is mostly survey program. Within each high-failure program the model ranks at ROC-AUC 0.51–0.62, and knowing only the provider gives ROC-AUC 0.83, close to the model's 0.87 (provider check). Its 3.8× lift should not be read as pump-level knowledge.
  • Accuracy barely beats "every point works" except in Tanzania (by under 1 point elsewhere). The models can order visits, but their yes/no calls are no better than assuming every point works.
  • Some countries have few test districts (Tanzania 16, Ethiopia 33), so intervals are wide and one district can move a result. Ethiopia's largest district (29.3% of rows) is never tested.
  • There are no environmental features. Rainfall, groundwater depth and conflict are absent; the error analysis shows this is where the biggest misses are.
  • Static attributes and the label come from the same visit, so nothing here says when a working pump will fail. This is a cross-sectional ranking, not a forecast.
  • We made two amendments after the first run. Regime C (6 Nov) and the country-aware pooled models (15 Nov) were not planned in advance. Both are disclosed with reasons that do not depend on test results, and neither changed the test set, features, tuning or final-model choice.
  • We made many comparisons. After a Holm correction over 48 regime comparisons, 10 of 16 local-vs-foreign gaps stay significant; the direction of every conclusion holds, but single model-by-country gaps are less certain than their raw intervals suggest.
