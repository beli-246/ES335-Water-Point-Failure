Will this water point still work?

We rank about 157,000 rural water points in Uganda, Kenya, Tanzania and Ethiopia by their risk of having no water on the survey day, using only attributes that do not depend on the point's current status. Then we ask: how much of that skill is lost when the model has never seen the country it is used in?

ES 335 Machine Learning, IIT Gandhinagar · Beli Dedhia & Poojan Soni · November 2026 · Code on GitHub

Main findings
  • The model works, modestly. In all four countries it ranks water points better than random and better than a one-rule baseline. A crew that visits the riskiest 10% finds dry points 1.5–3.8 times as often as by visiting at random.
  • Local labels are what matter. Models trained only on other countries do much worse, and rank Ethiopia worse than random. Adding foreign data helps only if the model is told the country, and even then it only matches training on local data alone.
  • Read the numbers with care. Ethiopia's strong result mostly comes from telling survey programs apart. Accuracy beats "every point works" clearly only in Tanzania (elsewhere by under 1 point), so use these models to order visits, not to label points yes or no.

With local data, the riskiest 10% of points flagged by the final model have 1.5–3.8 times the overall no-water rate. A model trained only on other countries does much worse; in Ethiopia it ranks points backwards.

Terms used on this site
No-water rate
Share of points with no water on the survey day (9% in Kenya to 43% in Tanzania). A random ranking finds dry points at exactly this rate.
PR-AUC
Average precision of the ranking: how pure the top of the list is, averaged over all list lengths. Random = the no-water rate; perfect = 1.
Lift
No-water rate among the points a crew visits ÷ the overall rate. 2× means a visit is twice as likely to find a dry point as a random visit.
Skill
(PR-AUC − no-water rate) ÷ (1 − no-water rate). 0 = random, 1 = perfect, whatever the base rate, so countries can be compared.
ROC-AUC
Chance that a random dry point is ranked above a random working one. 0.5 = random.
Regimes A, B, C
What the model was trained on: A = other countries plus the target's own training districts; B = other countries only; C = the target's own training districts only.
Test districts
Whole districts, about 20% of each country, held out before any model was trained and scored once at the end.
95% interval
From resampling whole test districts 2,000 times; the range a result moves over if we had drawn different districts.
Calibration
Whether a predicted 30% risk really means 30% of such points are dry.
