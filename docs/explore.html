The pattern is the same in every country. Confident false alarms are mostly hand pumps with no or missing management, or pumps decades old: the profile that is usually risky. Confident misses are mostly points with a missing technology record, or brand-new taps and rainwater systems: the profile that is usually safe. Static attributes cannot tell these cases apart. A new tap can still run dry, and an old hand pump can still work.

Most of the remaining error sits at the level of whole districts. Averaged per test district (districts with at least 20 test points, as in the table above), predicted and observed no-water rates agree well in Ethiopia (correlation 0.88) and Kenya (0.83), moderately in Tanzania (0.69, from only 11 districts, so it moves a lot with which districts are included) and poorly in Uganda (0.25). Siti and Nogob are arid lowlands, where seasonal drying is one plausible reason; rainfall and groundwater are not in WPdx. The model flags Kajiado South as risky, but its points work. Missing management tracks the data provider, and providers differ between training and test districts.

This is the same spatial correlation that makes random CV optimistic: districts differ in ways the features cannot see. A district-level effect (for example, a hierarchical model) would need labelled points from the same district, and a new district has none.
