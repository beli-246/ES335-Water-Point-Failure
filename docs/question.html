The question

A district water office cannot visit every hand pump, borehole and tap each year. If it could rank points by risk, a crew with time for, say, 10% of points could go to the most likely failures first. The prediction target is WPdx's status_id = "No": no water at the point on the visit day. All four countries record it the same way, which is not true of the free-text status.

At prediction time the model sees only attributes that describe how a point was built and where it is: age, water source, technology, management type, local population, distances to roads and towns, and an urban flag. Age and remoteness are among the predictors earlier work linked to failed pumps [Foster 2013]. None of them measures whether water is flowing. In WPdx these attributes are recorded on the same survey visit as the label. Our use case therefore assumes that a ministry already holds them from an earlier inventory or construction record, and wants to decide where to send the next visit. The label is what the visit found. This is a cross-sectional ranking problem, not forecasting: each point appears once, at its latest survey.

That assumption has a risk. If survey teams record attributes differently when a point is dry (for example, leaving management blank), part of the signal would come from recording practice. Missing values are the most likely route for this, so the no-missing-signal ablation removes them, and the provider check measures how much of the ranking follows the survey.

We use four countries because ministries often have survey data from elsewhere but not from their own district. So the second question is about transfer: does a model trained on other countries' surveys rank points in a new country as well as one trained on local data?
